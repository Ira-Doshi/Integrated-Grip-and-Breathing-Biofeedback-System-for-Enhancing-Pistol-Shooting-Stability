Integrated Grip and Breathing Biofeedback System for Enhancing Pistol Shooting Stability

To effectively perform precision pistol shooting, the shooter must have exceptional stability, controlled breathing and consistent trigger pressure. However, even small imperfections such as a slight tremble, improper grip pressure or timing are major contributors in reducing the accuracy levels. All current training methods for shooters are subjective and do not provide a means of obtaining real-time, objective and quantitative data, hence there is little systematic need for improving a shooter's performance. This project seeks to develop an integrated, low-cost biofeedback system that combines motion sensing, physiological monitoring  as well as grip force monitoring analysis to improve the accuracy. The system utilizes an MPU6050 to measure the amount of tremor and tilt alignment, a MAX30102 sensor for heart rate and SpO₂ monitoring to help establish proper breathing phases, and multiple force sensitive resistors and flex sensors to evaluate grip consistency and trigger pressure. In addition to this, a real-time dashboard will provide an intuitive method for visualizing the shooter's stability, tilt direction, and shot detection during use, while a haptic feedback module will provide the shooter with feedback on their ability to maintain prolonged stable aiming along with a built in breathing exercise display with the 4-7-8 rule. Results from experimental testing show that using the biofeedback system will produce significant improvement in the stability of shooting with an average reduction in unintended hand movement by 35% while also greatly improving the consistency of shot timing during the exhalation phase. Therefore, the precision shooting biofeedback system is an effective training aide (data-driven) that improves the shooter's awareness and performance while also being scalable and accessible to those who want to improve their precision shooting.
